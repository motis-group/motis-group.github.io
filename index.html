The bottleneck was never the model.
It's the rules nobody wrote down.

A model can read every manual on site and still not know which conveyor trips in the wet, which feeder runs hot in a heatwave, or which of four spreadsheets holds the real tonnes. None of that reached a schema. It lives in a planner, a metallurgist or a network engineer, and it leaves when they do.

So I write it down first, in a form software can check. Deterministic rails for the stable core, and a model only where judgment is genuinely required.

Step 1

A read of the operation

For something small, read-only access and a short look. For anything larger or regulated, a fixed-price discovery against a named system.

What comes back names the systems, the tables and the people holding the exceptions. If the finding is that you don't need me, that's what it says.

Step 2

Write the operation down

Every asset, event and state gets one definition that the historian, the ERP and the maintenance system have to reconcile against. In energy that's CIM, IEC 61968 and 61970. In mining it starts from the asset hierarchy your maintenance system already carries.

assets/primary_crusher_01.yml
asset:      primary_crusher_01
kind:       crusher
historian:  PI    CR01.FEED_TPH
cmms:       SAP   FL-CR-01
rule:       state == RUNNING when feed_tph > 0
on_break:   fail the build, name the owner
Step 3

Rails first, then agents

Pipelines, tests and lineage, so every figure walks back to the sensor or record that produced it. Then agents in your cloud account, on your permissions. No migration: each system keeps its own records.

A pipeline fails at 3am and the agent finds the cause, writes the fix on a branch, runs the tests and opens a pull request for a person to sign.

Three engagements, and what stayed behind.

01

An ERP go-live pushed development compute up 842 hours in a week.

The question on the table was how much capacity to buy. I attributed every hour to a workload and an owner: 81% was scheduled model rebuilds colliding in one lane. The fix was lanes and schedules.

↳ The attribution queries stayed, as a playbook anyone on the team can rerun.

02

A new emissions standard put a penalty balance on every vehicle sold.

The only check lived in one analyst's spreadsheet. I wrote it down as a specification, then as a test suite: every rule applied by hand became an assertion that fails loudly when the warehouse disagrees.

↳ The test register, the daily status and sign-off all generate from one run.

03

A platform team had good models and no end-to-end test.

Testing meant dropping and recreating schemas, and one engineer held that access, so every pull request queued behind his calendar. I refactored seeds and snapshots so a build runs from committed fixtures, on anyone's branch.

Before the first read.

What to know before you book a call.

What happens on the first call?

You describe the operation, the number or the check. I ask which systems it touches and who holds the exceptions, then tell you whether it's a read-only look, a fixed-price discovery, or not a job for me.

Do we have to migrate anything?

No. The historian, the ERP, the maintenance system and GIS keep their own records. The schema is what joins them, and nothing moves to make it work.

What does the model actually touch?

Condensed extracts, never raw records at volume. It drafts on a branch and never writes to a system of record. Sensing and acting are code.

How much of my team's time does it take?

Mostly an afternoon with the person who holds the exceptions, while they are still here, then short reviews as the rules get written down.

How do you handle access and security?

Least privilege, read-only until there's a written reason for it not to be, and scoped to the systems named in the contract. Your data stays in your account and your region. If you have counsel, a CISO or an IT lead, I'd rather meet them in week one than hear from them in month three.

What happens when you leave?

Somebody on your team runs it without me. The tests, the runbook and the recordings stay. If your people can't operate it after I go, I built the wrong thing.

Have you done this inside an energy network?

Not yet as a delivery. My record is in mining and adjacent asset-heavy operations: vehicle distribution under a new federal standard, manufacturing, national retail supply. Energy networks are where the practice is heading, and the domain work is underway.

How does pricing work?

Fixed price, quoted before the build starts, and no retainer. If it runs harder than I estimated, I wear that. A discovery fee never comes back as credit against a build.
