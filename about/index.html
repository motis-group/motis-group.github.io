I find where a business is quietly relying on one person to catch its mistakes, and build the thing that catches them instead.
 
Somewhere in most operations a person, not a system, is the last check. They remember which supplier invoices late, which number looks wrong in March, which of the four reports is the real one. Nobody planned it. It grew around a gap.
That person is why things mostly work. They are also why nothing can be handed over, why nobody can take a month off, and why things drift for years before anyone notices.
 
Owners feel this. They rarely name it, because it does not present as a data problem. It presents as "Sarah does that", which holds right up until Sarah leaves or Sarah is wrong.
 
What I do:
⊹ read a system cold and find where the documented version and the working version came apart
⊹ write the rules down in a form software can enforce instead of a person remembering
⊹ build the checks that fail loudly, so nobody has to go looking
⊹ leave you the recordings and the runbook, so the next person does not start from zero
 
One question tells me most of what I need:
What does someone check by hand every week, and what happens the week they don't?
 
How it works:
⊹ Twenty minutes, free. Send me a login and I read the system cold, then tell you what I found whether or not there is a job in it.
⊹ Fixed price, scoped from what I found and quoted before anything starts. If it turns out harder than I estimated, that is mine to wear.
⊹ Anything I find outside the scope, I stop and quote separately. Nothing gets done you have not agreed to.
 
There is no monthly retainer, on purpose. Work priced by the month has to stay manual to stay billable, and the whole point is to delete the manual work. Occasional project work six months apart is fine. Being on your payroll to keep a problem alive is not.
